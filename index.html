Light is a kind of energy that travels as a wave. Most LED bulbs at home do not give a perfectly steady light. They switch brighter and darker 100 times every second. Our eyes cannot see this, but the brain may still notice it. Scientists think this hidden flicker can make some people feel tired, get headaches, or find it harder to sit still and focus. In this test, you read the same kind of text under two different lights, and a small motion sensor on your wrist counts how often your hand moves. If the hand moves more under the flickering light, the light may be making you more restless. Keep reading slowly and calmly until the timer ends.
Note: Hand movement shows restlessness only. It does not diagnose ADHD or any medical condition.

How it works

LED bulbs don't give perfectly steady light — many blink 100 times every second. It's too fast to see, but the eyes and brain still feel it (eye strain, tiredness, headaches).

FlickerScope: Light → TEMT6000 sensor → Raspberry Pi Pico (2000 readings/sec) → this app, which finds the flicker frequency, % flicker and gives an eye-safety rating (IEEE 1789-2015).

How to use
1. Plug the FlickerScope into the laptop with the USB cable.
2. Click ⚡ Connect FlickerScope, choose the port (usbmodem / USB Serial / COM) and press Connect.
3. Put the light to be tested in front of the scope tube.
4. Scroll down, type the light's name and click Save Reading. Repeat for other lights.
5. Click Download CSV to get all results in Excel.

Fidget Tracker: A small motion sensor (MPU-6050) on the wrist band tells the Pico how much the hand is moving. The app turns this into a Restlessness score and counts fidgets.
For the test: run Test 1 under a flickering bulb and Test 2 under a steady (flicker-free) light, then compare. Hand movement shows restlessness only; it is not a medical test.

Result guide: Green = no visible effect · Yellow = low risk · Red = high risk.
